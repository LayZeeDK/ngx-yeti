## Approach

- Reset: in `@layer ngx-yeti` of `packages/ngx-yeti/ngx-yeti-globals.css`, set `--_ngx-yeti-band-ink` to `var(--yeti-color-focus)` on every surface of the list in Context, so a ring inside one of them is Yeti's own ring again. Pitfall: the reset and the paint mapping can match one element (a `.card` that also carries `data-paint`); the paint must win there, so the reset either comes before the 20 paint lines at equal specificity or is wrapped in `:where()`. `.box[data-surface]` outranks a single attribute selector, so order alone does not hold for it. A nested band inside a surface sets its own ink again by inheritance; keep that.
- Toggle label: the ring rule also colours `[data-paint] .button:has(> input:focus-visible)` with the band ink, so the label's visible ring follows the band. A toggle inside a surface of the list keeps Yeti's ring through the reset.
- Keep T058's design: only `outline-color` changes, no `@media (forced-colors: active)` block, no Yeti token redefinition, no `--_yeti-*` token, no `@scope` (the Firefox 145 floor lacks it). Keep the header comment of the file and the A11Y-35 comment true; the comment says what the code cannot (why the surfaces reset), with no measurement, date, or task id.
- `packages/ngx-yeti/src/published-output.node.spec.ts`: the `'A11Y-35'` entry of `sanctionedSelectors` names every new selector (each surface and the toggle label).
- `packages/ngx-yeti/src/accessibility.node.spec.ts`: a check that lists the surfaces of Context and fails when the reset leaves one out or adds one the list does not name; the coder may also assert that each listed Yeti selector still appears in Yeti's CSS, so a pin move that renames one fails. If this adds a count pin, item 19's step names it too.
- `box--paint` (`packages/ngx-yeti/box/src/box.stories.ts`), in both schemes: a `yetiCard` (import from `ngx-yeti/card`, as `packages/ngx-yeti/lift/src/lift.stories.ts` does) inside a `primary` band holding a link; tab to the link and assert its ring at least 3:1 against the card's computed fill. A `label[yetiButton]` toggle (import from `ngx-yeti/button`) in a ring band; tab to its input and assert the label's ring at least 3:1 against the band. The ratio on the `button-band` button (`yetiText="primary"` on `primary`) against its band. The ring bands padded by at least 4px, so the whole ring lies on the band it is measured against. Keep every existing assertion; the band order checks extend to any new band.
- Show the story can fail, and record each run in the Log: once with the ring rule changed to `outline-color: currentColor` (expected to fail at the `button-band` button), and once with the surface reset removed (expected to fail at the card link), each restored afterwards.
- Records: ledger row A11Y-35 (`docs/specs/ledger.md`) names the surface reset and the toggle-label selector in its package column, says the ring reads on the band where it sits on the band (a band needs at least 4px of padding around a control for that), and its Verified and Tested-by columns name the new measurements and story cases. The Departures row for `box.md:207` and the globals paragraph of `.claude/skills/ngx-yeti-specs/SKILL.md` (which says the A11Y-35 selector names no Yeti class) are made true. Line 48 of `.claude/skills/ngx-yeti-accessibility/SKILL.md` (the pattern of a Yeti colour that equals its fill) adds that surfaces repainting inside the fill reset the copied colour; line 20 names the job's lanes (NVDA in Playwright's Chromium and Firefox), with Edge only as the extra lane, from the T027 measurement quoted in Context. Step 5 of `.claude/skills/yeti-pin/SKILL.md` lists `packages/ngx-yeti/src/accessibility.node.spec.ts` and its pin of Yeti's 20 `[data-paint]` rules (and any count pin this task adds). One CHANGELOG entry for the fix in `packages/ngx-yeti/CHANGELOG.md`.
- `docs/specs/` is otherwise immutable; only `ledger.md` changes here. `docs/specs/upstream-bugs.md` row Y16 describes Yeti alone and does not change.
- Engines: the ring colour can differ by engine, so the Verify runs the box stories in Chromium natively and in Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit through the `ci unit` container job.
